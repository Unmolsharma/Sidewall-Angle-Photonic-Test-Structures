## Why so many ratios are not robust

To first order in boundary-perturbation theory, tilting a sidewall by angle a is the same as changing the width by -h·tan(a) whenever the cross-section is mirror-symmetric top to bottom. Strip, slot and SWG are, in this model (oxide above and below). The ratio R = (dn/da) / (-h·tan 1° · dn/dw) should then be exactly 1:

| Family | R for n_eff, corrected (5 widths) | R for n_eff, original averaging |
|---|---|---|
| strip_TE | 0.9983 to 0.9995 | 0.9905 to 1.0155 |
| strip_TM | 1.0002 to 1.0104 | 0.9973 to 1.0484 |
| slot_TE | 1.0001 to 1.0130 | 0.9699 to 1.0254 |
| slot_TM | 0.9951 to 1.0000 | 0.9780 to 1.0136 |
| swg_TE | 0.9991 to 0.9996 | 0.9925 to 1.0106 |
| rib_TE | 0.8127 to 0.8229 | 0.7366 to 0.8887 |

For every symmetric structure, sidewall angle carries almost no information separate from width. The little that remains is a residual of order 0.1-1%, which is at or below the accuracy of the derivatives (see the step-size check and the mesh check below). Anything computed relative to a strip-only (or slot-only) baseline divides by that residual, so its magnitude is set by numerical error. The rib, whose slab breaks the symmetry, has R of about 0.82 (0.86 on the finest mesh), a real first-order difference. Quantities that depend on the rib's information directly, such as the angle precision with the rib included, are stable.

Thickness derivatives of TE strip, rib and slot TM are converged to about 1%. Width and angle derivatives move by 4-10% (strip TE, rib), 8-24% (strip TM) and up to 63% (slot TE) between meshes, non-monotonically. Cell-exact averaging conserves silicon, but it still spreads that silicon uniformly over a partly filled cell. That creates a kink in n_eff wherever a boundary crosses a cell edge (`diag_remaining_step_failures.py`). Removing it would need anisotropic (tensor) sub-pixel averaging or derivatives from a boundary-perturbation surface integral. Neither was done in this run.
