# Priors study and structure search (femwell r12 derivatives, no new solves)

Sidewall-angle Cramér-Rao bound (1-sigma, all other unknowns marginalized) of the **full (strip)
etch**, in degrees, at derivative step h, with min–max over h/2, h, 2h where shown. The parameters
and stages are those of `results_etchdepth_femwell/SUMMARY.md`:
- B: + etch depth
- C: + separate partial-etch (rib) angle
- D: + n_Si, n_ox
- E: + temperature

Rib noise is the conservative 1.0x unless stated. All tables are generated by
`src/priors_search_tables.py` into `data/summary_tables.md` (T1–T5) from the files written by
`src/priors_search.py`.

## Verdict (plain language)

**Under realistic priors, one rib makes the strip angle no better than the strip alone, and two
widths of that rib make it about 3.3x better.** The full model (Stage E) with realistic priors
gives:

| Set | Strip angle | vs strip alone |
|---|---|---|
| Strip alone | 1.39° | – |
| Strip + one rib (70 nm slab, 500 nm) | 1.38° | 1.0x |
| Strip + the same rib at 500 + 300 nm | 0.41° | 3.3x |

Source: T2. With the optimistic rib noise (0.2x), two rib widths give 0.12°.

- **What outside knowledge recovers the most:** the only prior that helps a single rib noticeably is
  **a link between the rib's and the strip's sidewall angle**. If they are known to differ by at
  most 1°, one rib gives 0.93° (1.5x); at 2° it gives 1.21° (1.16x); at 5° almost nothing (T1).
  Everything else is worth at most 7%:
  - etch depth to 1-5 nm;
  - n_Si, n_ox to 1e-3 or 1e-4;
  - temperature to 1 K.
- **Etch depth from ellipsometry does not help.** To recover the "depth known" precision, a single
  rib needs the depth to about 0.01 nm (Stage B, 0.0096° vs 0.0018° with depth known). At 0.3 nm
  it gives 0.21°, and at 1-5 nm nothing (T4). With a separate rib angle (Stage C), no depth prior
  helps a single rib at all (1.28° even at 0.01 nm).
- **Index priors only matter if temperature is also known.** In Stage E with temperature free,
  priors on n_Si and n_ox change nothing, because temperature takes up whatever index shift the data
  need (T1).
- **Best sets:**
  - With a separate rib angle (Stage C), the best 5-8 structure sets reach 0.145-0.151° using one
    partial etch: one to three TE strips, one TM strip and three or four widths of the 90 nm-slab rib.
  - The best strips-only set of the same size gives 0.61-0.68°, so a well-chosen set of rib widths
    is worth about 4x. The step spread for these sets is about −26% to +17%.
  - With index unknowns (Stage D) and realistic priors, 0.167-0.176° at 6-8 structures (T5).
- **Bottom line:** the earlier "~1000x" rib advantage is not recoverable by any outside measurement
  tested here. The realistic gain is about 3-4x, and it requires several widths of the same rib.

## Priors used

| Prior | Levels tested | "Realistic" choice | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Etch depth d (per partial etch) | 1, 2, 5 nm (sweep 0.01-5 nm in T4) | 2 nm | **assumption** (separate thickness measurement on a pad; no verified source) |
| n_Si | 1e-3, 1e-4 | 1e-4 | **sourced for bulk crystalline Si:** absolute index uncertainty 8.67e-5 at 1.5 µm, 295 K (Frey, Leviton, Madison, Proc. SPIE 6273, 62732J (2006), arXiv:physics/0606168, Table 1). Doping and stress in the SOI device layer are not covered |
| n_ox (BOX and cladding, one parameter) | 1e-3, 1e-4 | 1e-3 | **assumption.** Bulk fused silica is known to ±1e-5 (Leviton, Frey, arXiv:0805.0091, abstract), but deposited cladding oxide varies more; I found no verified source for how much |
| Rib angle | (a) free; (b) prior on (ar − a) of 1, 2, 5° | free | **assumption.** The two angles come from different etch steps |
| Temperature (Stages D–E) | 1 K | 1 K | **assumption** (stage with temperature control); not in the request, added because in Stage E index priors are useless without it |
| "all optimistic" | d 1 nm, n_Si and n_ox 1e-4, T 0.1 K, ar − a 1° | – | upper bound on what priors can give |

Priors enter as F_total = F + diag(1/σ²), implemented as one pseudo-observation row per prior. For
the angle link the row is (e_ar − e_a)/σ.

## Structure search (source: `data/search.json`, `data/search_table.csv` via `data/summary_tables.md`)

**Method:**
- **Candidates:** each candidate is one structure read out by a ring (n_eff, n_g) and an MZI (phase).
- **Pool:** every structure with the derivatives the stage needs.
  - Stage C, 18 candidates: strip TE and TM at 400–600 nm; rib TE at 70 and 90 nm slab, 300–600 nm.
  - Stage D, 14 candidates: strip TM only at 500 nm, the only TM width with index derivatives.
- **Search:** exhaustive over all sets of 1–8 structures, which is cheap at this pool size, plus the
  requested greedy selection.
- **Variants:**
  - any slabs;
  - one partial etch (ribs from a single slab, since two slabs need two etch steps);
  - strips only (the fair baseline for the rib's contribution).
- **Ranking:** by the strip-angle CRB at h, conservative rib noise.

**Stage C, no priors (T5-C-none).** The realistic-prior table is identical from k = 3 upward
(T5-C-realistic).

|---|---|---|---|

| 3 | rib 400 s70 | 1.56° | 0.023 |

| 5 | rib 500 s70 | 0.408° | 0.32 |

| 7 | rib 600 s70 | 0.196° | 1.4 |
| 8 | TE 550 | 0.195° | 1.4 |

**Reading:**
- The angle becomes identifiable only once there are two rib widths plus a strip.
- The second strip polarization (TM) gives the next large step.
- Then each extra rib width and strip width gives diminishing returns.
- Greedy selection ends worse than exhaustive search (0.195° vs 0.167° at 8 structures). Its first
  picks have no finite CRB, so it can only rank them by numerical rank.
- Exhaustive search prefers the 90 nm slab at 5–8 structures and the 70 nm slab at 4.

**Strips only (Stage C):**
- Three TM strips (400/450/550 nm) give 0.786°, better than the five TE strips (1.33°).
- TM-heavy strip sets are the strongest strips-only option. Their step spread is large, though
  (0.345–0.614° at k = 8).
