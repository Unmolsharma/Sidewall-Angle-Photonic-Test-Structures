\subsection{Monte Carlo on femwell data}
\label{sec:surrcheck}

The fits above take both the data and the model from the surrogate, so they cannot show what the surrogate's own
errors do to the estimate. For each of the two sets we drew 20 parameter vectors from the CRLB covariance, solved
every structure at each of them with femwell on the r12 mesh (160 solves for the best set, 140 for the two-width set),
and used these mode indices as noiseless data. Each data set received 25 noise realizations at
\qty{1}{pm}, giving 500 fits per set, all made with the surrogate as the model and the settings above.

At these geometries the surrogate follows the strips to within $0.8627\,\sigma_\phi$ in $\neff$, and its $\ngr$ errors
stay below $0.0524\,\sigma_{\ngr}$. The \qty{300}{nm} ribs are again the weak point. On the \qty{50}{nm} slab their
$\neff$ misses femwell by $5.166\,\sigma_\phi$ RMS and by $12.42\,\sigma_\phi$ at worst, and on the \qty{90}{nm} slab
by $1.519\,\sigma_\phi$ and $3.366\,\sigma_\phi$.

The two-width set does not pass these errors on to the angle. Its fitted angles scatter by \qty{0.4034}{\degree}
(\qtyrange{0.3781}{0.4273}{\degree}, 95\% bootstrap interval), 1.037 times the CRLB, with a mean error of
\qty{0.02463 \pm 0.01804}{\degree}, and all 500 fits converge inside the box. The best set of eight does not reach its
bound on femwell data. Its spread is \qty{0.2067}{\degree} (\qtyrange{0.1859}{0.2253}{\degree}), 1.487 times the
CRLB of \qty{0.139}{\degree}, and 14 fits end at the box edge, even though the surrogate errors on its
\qty{300}{nm} rib are the smaller of the two.

To locate this gap we repeated the 500 fits with the same noise draws and the data of some structures taken from the surrogate.
Replacing only the \qty{300}{nm} rib with surrogate data leaves the spread at \qty{0.1988}{\degree}
(\qtyrange{0.1785}{0.218}{\degree}), and keeping femwell data for that rib alone gives \qty{0.1664}{\degree}, no more than the
\qty{0.1689}{\degree} obtained with surrogate data everywhere. The data error of the \qty{300}{nm} rib therefore does not explain the gap, and for the two-width set
the much larger error of its \qty{300}{nm} rib has no effect either (\qty{0.4023}{\degree} against \qty{0.4034}{\degree}). Two other contributions remain. With surrogate data
everywhere the spread is already 1.215 times the CRLB at these perturbed truths, against 0.952 times at the nominal point, so part of the gap appears without any
model error, away from the nominal parameters. The rest, from \qty{0.1689}{\degree} to \qty{0.2067}{\degree}, requires femwell data for the other structures, among which
the 400 to \qty{600}{nm} ribs have the largest surrogate errors, 0.329 to $0.613\,\sigma_\phi$ RMS. With 500 fits these intervals overlap, so the split between the two is approximate.
This test does not address the effect of the \qty{300}{nm} rib derivatives on the bound itself, which moves by \qty{-15.3}{\percent} with fixed-mesh
derivatives (Section~\ref{sec:multi}). We take \qty{0.39}{\degree} for the two-width set as the result that holds with an independent forward model, and
report the best set as a CRLB of \qty{0.139}{\degree} shown beside its \qty{0.2067}{\degree} spread on femwell data.
